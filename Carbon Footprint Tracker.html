    Venture brief

    Problem Definition

    1. What sustainability challenge are you trying to solve?

    Individuals and small businesses cannot easily track or reduce their carbon emissions, because current carbon accounting tools are expensive and designed for large enterprises.

    2. Why is this challenge important locally and globally?

    Locally, energy bills, supply chains, and buyer requirements are creating a greater need for emissions data. Globally, halving emissions this decade to keep warming near 1.5 °C depends heavily on the everyday decisions of ordinary people and smaller firms.

    3. Which SDGs are addressed?

    This idea directly supports SDG 12 (Responsible Consumption and Production), SDG 13 (Climate Action), SDG 9 (Industry, Innovation and Infrastructure), SDG 8 (Decent Work and Economic Growth), SDG 11 (Sustainable Cities and Communities), and SDG 17 (Partnerships for the Goals).

    Innovation and Sustainability

    4. What makes your idea unique?

    Unified tracking. It provides a single platform for personal and business carbon footprints. Emissions are logged by activity (electricity, distribution, marketing, and materials) and shown as changes over time and per dollar of revenue, instead of in a once-a-year report.

    5. How does it create environmental benefits?

    Actionable visibility. Hotspot tips, daily budgets, and trend tracking help users target their biggest emission sources.

    6. How does it create social benefits?

    Democratized access and literacy. It builds climate literacy, involves both staff and customers, and equips small businesses with tools typically reserved for large corporations.

    7. How does it generate economic value?

    The platform combines sustainability and ESG (Environmental, Social, and Governance) consulting with software. It creates value in three ways:
    • Cost reduction: cuts operating expenses by optimizing energy use, shipping logistics (freight), and raw materials.
    • Compliance and readiness: prepares companies to meet regulatory standards and to satisfy corporate buyers who demand supply-chain sustainability data.
    • Funding access: helps businesses qualify for green loans, sustainability-linked financing, and government grants.
    Business Model

    8. Who are your customers?

    Customer segments: everyday individuals; small and medium-sized businesses (specifically in fashion, retail, food, and logistics); sustainability consultants; and employers or municipalities managing group programs.

    9. What is your value proposition?

    Users can quickly track, lower, and verify their carbon emissions without hiring a specialist.

    10. How will you generate revenue?

    Revenue streams: a freemium model with a free personal tier, paid business subscriptions (scaled by company size), paid audit-ready reports, and referral commissions from verified emission-reduction partners.

    11. What are your major costs?

    Cost structure: software development and hosting, maintaining up-to-date emission-factor data, verifying methodologies, customer support, and marketing.

    Environmental Assessment

    12. What resources are required?

    The platform relies on digital infrastructure only: cloud hosting, emission-factor databases, and developer hours. No physical hardware is required upfront, although optional smart meters may be integrated later.

    13. How will you minimize waste and emissions?

    Operational sustainability. The platform limits its own digital carbon footprint by using lightweight web pages, hosting data in regions powered by renewable energy, and removing the need for users to buy physical devices for basic tracking.

    14. What circular economy principles are included?

    Circular economy support. The platform helps businesses reduce waste by tracking virgin versus recycled materials, identifying landfill waste, suggesting circular alternatives (reuse, repair, rental, and take-back), and connecting firms with recycling and resale partners.

    Stakeholder Analysis

    15. Who are the key stakeholders?

    Users, employees, suppliers, customers, investors, regulators, NGOs, municipalities, and auditors.

    16. How will governments, NGOs, and communities support the project?

    Governments can contribute through grants, procurement, and standards. NGOs can assist with methodology reviews, training, and outreach. Communities can participate through local pilots and campaigns.

    Policy and Governance

    17. What regulations or policies affect your idea?

    Compliance and rules: carbon pricing, emissions disclosures, supply-chain data requests, environmental-claims (greenwashing) regulations, and user data privacy laws.

    18. Are there potential green taxes, subsidies, or incentives that could help?

    Financial incentives: opportunities vary by region and include carbon-tax savings, energy-efficiency rebates, small-business clean-tech grants, and green loans.

    Impact Assessment

    19. How will success be measured?

    Success is defined by sustained user logging combined with decreasing emissions over time.

    20. What environmental, social, and economic indicators will be used?

    • Environmental: kg CO₂e per user, percent change, kg per $1,000 of revenue, and waste diverted.
    • Social: active users, engaged employees, small businesses reached, and training delivered.
    • Economic: identified cost savings, subscription revenue, user retention, and grants secured.
    Scaling Strategy

    21. Can the solution be replicated elsewhere?

    Adaptability. The tool can be adapted to other industries and regions by updating emission factors, adding languages, and creating industry-specific templates.

    22. What challenges might arise during implementation?

    Challenges and solutions. Key hurdles include data accuracy, the effort of manual logging, greenwashing risks, competing tools, verification funding, and varying regulations. These can be addressed by integrating billing and accounting software and by using a transparent, public methodology.
